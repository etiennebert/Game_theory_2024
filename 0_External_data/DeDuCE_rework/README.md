## How the numbers are built

1. DeDuCE commodity → GTAP land-using activity: cereals → `pdr`/`wht`/`gro`; fruit, vegetables, roots, pulses → `v_f`; oilseeds and oleaginous fruits (soy, palm fruit, coconut…) → `osd`; sugar crops → `c_b`; fibre crops → `pfb` except rubber, tobacco, mint, pyrethrum → `ocr`; stimulants/spices (cocoa, coffee, tea…) → `ocr`; pasture (cattle meat + leather) → the three grazing activities `ruminant`, `rmk`, `wol` in proportion to their AEZ land rents (Brazil 69 / 29 / 2 %), so dairy and wool carry their share without double counting; forest plantations excluded.
2. DeDuCE ISO → model region via the `MREG` mapping in `sets.har` (residual regions by UN group; Myanmar sits in `xse`).
3. `D(a, r)` = mean annual attributed hectares over the window. Main case: **amortised (5-yr), 2018–2023** = "embodied in production" in the Pendrill/DeDuCE convention. Also provided: unamortised, and 2012–2019 for comparison with your HILDA+ results.
4. `h_direct(a, r) = D / VOM` (ha per $, VOM = column sum of MAKS). `h_total = h_direct × (I − A)^-1` with `A` the domestic intermediate-use coefficients from VDFB, **own-use diagonal set to zero** (otherwise DRC `pdr`, with 98% own-use in GTAP, inflates by ×50). This carries cattle → beef, raw milk → dairy, soy/palm → vegoil, cane → sugar, paddy → milled rice, hides → leather.
5. `AVE = p_ha × h_total` (fractions). Carbon variant: `AVE = P_CO2 × e_total`, `e_total` from DeDuCE emissions incl. peat drainage, negative values floored at 0 (Ivory Coast cocoa agroforestry is net negative in DeDuCE).
6. Bilateral shock: `100 × AVE / (1 + t0)`, `t0 = VMSB/VCIF − 1` from `basedata.har`. Matters: EU MFN on beef is 32%, sugar 23%, rice 21%.

## Orders of magnitude (amortised 2018–2023)

## EUDR baseline (`eudr_cut_shares.xlsx`, used by `1_Code/Baseline_EUDR.R`)

The baseline is the EU alone applying the EUDR: its imports of the covered products from each origin fall by `s_u`, the share of that origin's output of the sector that comes from land deforested after the cut-off (31 Dec 2020). `s_u` = DeDuCE unamortised attribution 2021–2023 (ha, cumulative) ÷ the sector's land base in the GTAP-AEZ base data (harvested area `AREA` for `ocr`, `osd`; `Pastureland` cover `LCOV` for cattle). Covered: cattle meat + leather → `ruminant`, `RuminantMeat`; cocoa, coffee, natural rubber → `ocr`; soya, oil palm → `osd`, `vegoil`. GTAP `lea` is mostly footwear (outside the EUDR) and is not cut; wood is not attributed by DeDuCE and is not covered. Sheet `shocks` = the 94 EU import flows with `s_u ≥ 0.1 %` and positive base imports ($23.7 bn of EU imports); the other sheets show the ingredients. Orders of magnitude: Brazil pasture 2.6 %, Brazil soy 0.6 %, Indonesia palm 1.1 %, Ivory Coast `ocr` 6.2 %, DRC `ocr` 14.6 %; value-weighted cut on EU imports: `ocr` 1.6 %, `vegoil` 0.7 %, `RuminantMeat` 0.4 %. `eudr_scale` in the script multiplies the shares (traceability scenario: unverifiable output also excluded).

A ban, not a tax: for each cut flow `swap qxs(c,r,"eu27") = ams(c,r,"eu27")`, `shock qxs = −100·s_u`; the model solves the iceberg import cost that produces the cut, no tariff revenue. The solved `ams` is saved (`ams_baseline.txt`) and the game script (V16, `eudr_in_loop = "outsiders"`) keeps it as an exogenous cost on non-members' exports to the EU; members are deemed compliant.

## Business-as-usual clearing (`bau_deforestation.xlsx`, V21)

A comparative-static model has no deforestation in its baseline, so a "no net loss" commitment has nothing to prevent: a member exempt from the charge expands its agriculture and is pinned at 0, an outsider under the charge contracts and its forest grows, and the model concludes the forest does better with Brazil outside. V21 puts the observed clearing into the baseline. Sheet `cells`: for every region with ≥ 10 kha/yr of DeDuCE attribution (29 regions, 175 region × AEZ cells), the 2018–2023 mean annual clearing (unamortised, all commodities except forest plantations) × the horizon, spread over the region's AEZs in proportion to their Forestland cover (AEZs with forestry rent only), capped at 25 % of the cell's cover over the horizon — GTAP-AEZ's Forestland is far smaller than FAO forest where clearing is dry forest or savanna (Paraguay is the capped case). Five years: 22 Mha, Brazil 7.8 Mha (5.1 % of its cover), DRC 2.4, Bolivia 1.2, SEAsia 1.2, Ivory Coast 1.05 (19 %), Colombia 0.8, Indonesia 0.8.

**V34 (23 Oct 2026): the clearing is allocated to the AEZs by the observed clearing, not by forest cover.** `zenodo_v2.1\build_deduce_tables.py` now writes the workbook (and `eudr_cut_shares.xlsx`, reproduced to the last digit): the same DeDuCE 2018–2023 totals by region, split across the region's AEZs in proportion to the HILDA+ v2.1 conversions of forest (classes 40–45; plus unmanaged grass/shrubland, class 55, for Argentina, whose Chaco dry forest HILDA+ keeps outside its FRA-sized forest class) to annual crops, tree crops, agroforestry and pasture over 2012–2019, by country × AEZ (`HILDA_v2.1_transitions_country_AEZ.csv`, built by `hilda_country_aez_tables.py` from the HILDA+ netCDF files: every 0.01° cell assigned to a country and to the AEZ of the FAO GAEZ 18-class map — thermal class 1 tropical, 2–18 temperate, 19+ boreal, six growing-period classes — the map the model's GTAP-AEZ land cover follows). The clearing lands on the model's forest cells (Forestland cover and forestry rent > 0); the shares of AEZs without such a cell are spread over the cells (DRC: 52 % of its HILDA+ clearing is in AEZ4, which has no forest with rent); a cell never loses more than 25 % of its cover, the excess going to the region's other cells (water-filling; Paraguay: 0.18 Mha no cell can take). Sheets `hilda_shares` and `hilda_countries` carry the shares and the country mapping; `bau_shares_override.csv` (region, aez, share, source), if present, replaces the HILDA+ shares of the regions it lists — it does so for Brazil, where HILDA+ puts 13 % of the clearing in the temperate south (the 2013–2016 cropland statistics landing on forest cells of Paraná, São Paulo and Mato Grosso do Sul) while MapBiomas puts the Mata Atlântica and Pampa at 1.4 %: Brazil's shares are the MapBiomas RAD 2025 deforestation by biome, 2019–2023 (`mapbiomas_rad2025_by_biome.csv`; Amazônia 51 %, Cerrado 39 %, Caatinga 6 %, Pantanal 2 %), distributed over the AEZs inside each biome with the HILDA+ pattern of that biome (`HILDA_v2.1_transitions_brazil_biomes_AEZ.csv`, from `hilda_country_aez_tables.py --zones biomes_2019_simplified.gpkg`, the IBGE 2019 biomes) — AEZ5 51 %, AEZ6 26 %, AEZ4 20 %, AEZ12 2 % (`mapbiomas_rad2025_README.txt`). Options of the script: `--hilda-classes forest|forest55`, `--hilda-55-for ARG`, `--hilda-window 2012 2019`, `--bau-cap 25`, `--bau-horizon 5`, `--bau-min 10000`, `--no-bau`, `--no-eudr`. Brazil (HILDA+ alone, replaced by the override above): AEZ5 47 %, AEZ6 35 %, AEZ12 13 %, AEZ4 5 % (the cover shares gave every cell 5.07 %); Indonesia AEZ6 99 %; Bolivia 39 / 36 / 16 % in AEZ6 / 5 / 4; Colombia AEZ6 81 %; Peru AEZ6 72 %, AEZ12 21 %. HILDA+ is a FAO-harmonised reconstruction, not a tree-cover-loss product: its country totals sit between 0.2 and 8 times DeDuCE's and only the within-region shares are used; the earlier Alteryx match of the same data (`Forest_Club_SI\1. Data\1. HILDA data\3. Split_AEZ`) gives the same counts within 1–2 %.

`Baseline_EUDR.R` (bau_horizon = 5) imposes it with `swap p_LANDCOVER_L(AEZ,"Forestland",r) = tinc(AEZ,"forest",r); shock p_LANDCOVER_L = −x`, and saves the solved `tinc*` per cell (`bau_baseline.txt`): the implicit clearing incentive, a positive wedge on forest-land income (in $/ha: tinc % × forestry land income ÷ forest area). In the game (V21) non-members keep `shock tinc = tinc*` with their forest endogenous (leakage can happen); members are pinned at 0 (no net loss), whichever region they are. The commitment is then the avoided BAU clearing, it has a cost (the forgone expansion), and the solved member `tinc` (negative) is the payment that holds the forest. One solve per iteration; results in `forest_<price>_tau<tau>_eudr_bau5`. Forest can only be converted to crop or pasture land of the same AEZ (equation `E_areaadjust`: the three productive covers of an AEZ sum to a fixed area), so a cell with no crop or pasture cover cannot lose forest — pinning its cover makes the model structurally singular (US AEZ16, 270 ha of BAU clearing). `Baseline_EUDR.R` drops such cells and prints the largest clearing relative to the cell's crop + pasture area (US AEZ15, 107 %: a 36 kha loss into 33 kha of agriculture; every tropical cell is below 35 %). Sensitivity: rerun the baseline with bau_horizon = 1 and 10.

## Starting coalitions and the stability check (V22)

Who is inside at iteration 1 is now a run parameter, `seed` — `"eu27"`, `"eu27,brasil,argentina,paraguay,uruguay"`, the same plus `china` — instead of the threshold sheet's "everyone whose baseline EV is below −threshold_down" (`seed = "auto"` keeps that). From the seed the join/leave rule is untouched: every region decides at the same time, the thresholds are the moving cost, the loop stops when nobody moves (`dynamics = "on"`); `dynamics = "off"` solves the seed once and stops. `friction = "sheet"` reads the approach's thresholds as before; a number makes both thresholds a uniform share of GDP (`0.0001` = 0.01 % of GDP: Brazil 187 M$, China 1.5 bn$, EU 1.6 bn$, Ivory Coast 6 M$), which is easier to defend in a paper than four hand-made sheets.

`stability = TRUE` then asks whether the coalition S the loop stopped at is a Nash equilibrium of the membership game (d'Aspremont et al. 1983): every test starts again from S and moves one region — each member alone out (the EU excepted), each outsider alone in — one solve per test, nothing else changes. S is internally stable if no member gains by leaving, externally stable if no outsider gains by joining; the test does not depend on the order because every test is against the same S. `Stability_tests.txt` (also `4_DB/Stability_<approach>_forest_<run_tag>.txt` with the summary) gives per region: EV in the baseline, in S and after the deviation, the gain from moving, the same test with the threshold as a moving cost, the transfer that would keep a leaver, the world EV and the tropical forest change under the deviation (leakage), and the duty the charge raises on each member's imports from outsiders (`VMSB × s/(100 + s)` from `gdata.upd`, the updated data of the S solve — a comparison point for the transfers, not a welfare number). One solve per region ≈ 40 min per run in fast mode; `stability_scope` restricts the outsiders tested.

`Run_overnight.R` now crosses seeds and configurations: 3 seeds × (coalition 400, coalition 2 000, club 400 + 2 %, club 400 + 5 %) = 12 runs with `Realistic_wi_china` thresholds. 10 000 $/ha, the carbon variant and `tau_scope = "all"` are left out of the first night.

## Non-members in the game and solver accuracy (V23)

The accurate baseline (RunGTAP settings: Gragg 2‑4‑6, automatic accuracy, 3 subintervals; 5 min 29 s; accuracy face 9/10) gives clearing incentives `tinc*` that are all positive, median 36 %, Brazil 124–237 %, but astronomically large where the model's land market has no reason to clear at all: Ivory Coast AEZ6 52 396 %, Ghana AEZ6 12 548 %, Ivory Coast AEZ4/5 ≈ 6 100 %, US AEZ15 5 981 %. Re-imposing such wedges as shocks in every game iteration (the V21 mechanism, `bau_outsiders = "tinc"`) cannot be solved by the multi-step method, so V23 carries non-members on the BAU **cover path** instead (`bau_outsiders = "cover"`): the same `swap p_LANDCOVER_L = tinc; shock p_LANDCOVER_L = −x` lines as the baseline, which the solver handles. The price: a non-member's forest is no longer endogenous, so leakage is not measured in hectares; instead `Forest_pressure_<i>.txt` gives, per non-member cell, the wedge the model needs to hold the BAU path under the coalition against `tinc*` — a ratio below 1 means the coalition pushes that cell's market towards *more* clearing than BAU (leakage pressure), above 1 towards less. Leakage in hectares needs the game to start from the baseline's updated database (the wedges then sit in the data and are not shocked); parked for now.

The fast solver setting (one subinterval, no accuracy iteration) is not usable for these cover shocks: on the baseline it scored face 4/10, with Ghana's incentives at −2.5 million %, Ghana's EV at +3.3 bn$ and 132 range warnings. V23 therefore reads the accuracy face number of every solve from the solver log, prints it with the solve time, keeps it in `Solve_quality.txt`, redoes a solve below `min_face` (7) with the accurate settings, and checks every BAU cell against its target. Accurate solves take 5–6 minutes; a run of the dynamics is 3 to 10 solves.

## Productivity of the cleared hectares (`Calibrate_BAU_productivity.R`, V24)

The first V23 runs showed the flaw of the cover-only BAU: the clearing moves hectares out of forest, but the land the receiving activities use in production — `qes(AEZ, act, r)`, allocated by the land CET on 2017 rent shares — rises far less than the hectares (Ivory Coast AEZ6: pasture hectares +73 %, land used by grazing −0.2 %, real GDP −0.83 % when it clears; Brazil AEZ6: hectares +12 %, land used +7 %). Clearing destroys value in the model, so a member that stops clearing *gains* (Brazil +916 M$/yr, Ivory Coast +716 in the EU + Mercosur seed) — the opposite of the paper's premise. `Calibrate_BAU_productivity.R` gives the cleared hectares a stated productivity: in every BAU cell the cleared hectares are split across the activities DeDuCE attributes the region's clearing to (`h_table_DeDuCE.xlsx`, `D_unamort_w2018_2023`; an activity takes at most half its own land in the cell, activities under 1 000 ha are skipped), and each receiving activity's land services `qes + afeall` must rise by at least T = eta × 100 × hectares ÷ its land in the cell (`AREA` for crops, `Pastureland` cover split by `EVOS` rent shares for ruminant / rmk / wol), eta = 0.66 = the model's own yield of newly converted cropland (`ETA` in `E_harvstaez`). `afeall(AEZ, act, r)` is the land-augmenting technical change of `E_qfe`, exogenous in the closure; it is calibrated by iteration on the baseline solve itself (afeall ← max(0, T − qes), one accurate solve per pass, 3–4 passes) and written to `bau_afeall_eta0.66.txt`: 1 026 shocks, median 0.6 %, 90 % of them below 11 %, none above 33 %. Where the CET already gives an activity more land than T nothing is taken away. `Baseline_EUDR.R` (`bau_eta = 0.66`) adds the shocks to its solve, writes to `Baseline_EUDR_bau5_eta0.66/` and checks the shortfall; V24 gives non-members their shocks with their cover path and members neither — a member's commitment now costs the forgone expansion. Sensitivity: eta = 1 and 0.5 are one calibration and one baseline each. What the fix does not change: Brazil's baseline loss is mostly terms of trade (EV −960, GDP −0.02 %) — a large exporter gains from restraining supply in any CGE, and that stays.

## A uniform friction on the charged trade (V25, `Run_overnight_friction.R`)

The `Realistic_wi_china` sheet is itself a GDP rule: the leave threshold is a share of GDP rising with HDI (0.01 % to 0.03 %) capped at 500 M$ ("Thresholds Summit"), and the join threshold is that number times a diplomatic multiplier (Friendly 0.25, Truly Neutral 1, Conflicted √2, Tepid 4, Hostile 10⁷; China "Very Friendly" 0.04, hence its 20 M$ join threshold and its joining on a −77 M$ change). The V22 numeric friction removed the cap and the multipliers but kept GDP as the base, which cannot work: at 0.01 % of GDP China's threshold is 1 464 M$, the US 2 138, the EU 1 569, while the club moves China's EV by tens of M$ — the large economies would never move and the small ones would move at will. V25 measures the friction on what the region has at stake in the game, its **trade in the 17 charged commodities**: exports (`VFOB`, every destination) + imports (`VCIF`, every source) of the tms_f sectors from `2_Model/in/basedata.har`, in the data base's own $M (the units of EV), the intra-region trade of the composite regions excluded (`VFOB(c, r, r)` is 65 % of the EU's exports of these goods and the club never charges it). Both thresholds of a region = max(`friction_floor`, `friction` × base), the same for joining and leaving; `friction_floor` (2 M$) is the resolution of the game. At `friction = 0.001` (0.1 % of the charged trade): EU 227 M$, China 215, US 196, SEAsia 104, Brazil 73, India 50, Indonesia 43, Argentina 37, Malaysia 26, Ivory Coast 6.9, Uruguay 5.0, Nigeria 4.9, Paraguay 4.8, Ghana 4.0, DRC / Madagascar / Bolivia at the floor (`friction_trade_thresholds.csv` has every region at 0.05, 0.1 and 0.2 %). The rule reproduces the sheet for the small forest countries (Ivory Coast 6, Ghana 8.4, Paraguay 7.4) and replaces the 20 / 500 of China and the 411 / 581 of Brazil by one number each; China would then join only when a club hurts it by more than about 200 M$, which the 5 % and 7 % penalties on its 19 bn$ of charged exports into the EU can do and the footprint charge alone cannot. `friction_base = "gdp"` keeps the V22 base; `friction = "sheet"` is unchanged (checked with the mocked solver: V25 reproduces the V24 run folder and csv file for file, plus `Thresholds_used.txt`). With a numeric friction the thresholds no longer depend on the approach, so the runs go to `3_Output/Friction/` and `run_tag` gets `_fr<friction><base>`; every run folder now has `Thresholds_used.txt` (region, threshold_leave, threshold_join, and the base under a numeric friction), for the sheet runs as well. `Run_overnight_friction.R` is the twin of the sheet night — the same 3 seeds × 7 configurations at 0.1 % (21 runs, ≈ 9 h) — followed by 0.05 % and 0.2 % on the EU seed for the coalition at 400 $/ha, the club at 400 + 5 % and the carbon charge (6 runs). For the paper: the sheet stays the "Realistic" scenario with its diplomatic layer, the uniform friction is the mechanical benchmark with one parameter and its sensitivity.

## Caveats to carry into the SI

- DRC and Ivory Coast attributions are mostly statistical (quality index ≈ 0.2); Brazil soy 0.87, Argentina soy 0.80, Indonesia/Malaysia palm 0.6–0.7. Report the IQI sheet.
- Indonesia's attributed deforestation fell from 587 kha/yr (2012–19) to 265 kha/yr (2018–23); Argentina and Paraguay halved; Brazil rose. The window choice is a result, show both.
- The charge is origin-neutral: LatinAmer, SEAsia, CAfr, WAfr, Madagascar, Nigeria, Mozambique, Venezuela, Uruguay, Mexico… get their own (small) rates where DeDuCE attributes deforestation, and `shock_outsiders_into_members` applies them. Only the twelve producers can take the forest commitment (they are the ones with AEZ coefficients); everyone else can join as a consumer.
- `rmk` and `wol`: charged, at their land-rent share of pasture deforestation (not at the cattle rate as before, which double-counted the hectares).
- Peat drainage emissions are in the carbon variant; they are ongoing emissions from existing plantations, not new clearing — say so if you use that variant.
